Delivery Call-Off (DCO)

Master scheduling agreements, delivery releases, and the horizon-based visibility system that enables just-in-time supply chains.

Overview

What is DCO?

Delivery Call-Off is used when a buyer and supplier have a long-term supply relationship defined by a Scheduling Agreement (SA) — a framework contract that sets prices, terms, and total quantities over a period. Instead of individual POs, the buyer issues periodic Delivery Schedule Lines (call-offs) that tell the supplier exactly what to deliver and when.

This is fundamental to automotive manufacturing (JIT, JIS) where production lines need precise, timed material deliveries. JD transmits these schedules from SAP to suppliers and gives full horizon visibility.

Delivery Horizons

The Three Horizon Types

Delivery schedules are split into zones based on how firm the commitment is. Understanding these zones is critical for both buyer and supplier planning.

🔴 Fixed Horizon (Firm Zone)

Confirmed deliveries for production — quantities and dates are locked. Suppliers have already ordered materials based on this.

Typically 1–4 weeks out

🟡 Trade-off Horizon (Semi-firm)

Changes possible but require negotiation — supplier may have committed some materials. Both sides must agree before changes take effect.

Typically 4–8 weeks out

🟢 Forecast Horizon (Planning Zone)

Indicative quantities for planning only — not commitments. Suppliers use this to plan capacity and raw material procurement.

Typically 8–26 weeks out

Terminology

Key DCO Terms

Scheduling Agreement (SA)
Long-term supply contract in SAP (ME31L) defining item, vendor, plant, price, and total quantity over a time period.
DELFOR
Delivery Forecast IDOC/EDI message — the primary message transmitted from SAP to JD for scheduling agreement delivery lines.
DELJIT
Delivery Just-In-Time instruction — very short-term, precise call-offs used in automotive for exact sequence delivery.
JIT / JIS
Just-In-Time / Just-In-Sequence. JIS = materials arrive in the exact build sequence they'll be used on the production line.
Backlog
Delivery schedule lines whose date has passed but delivery hasn't occurred — flagged as overdue in JD.
Call-Off
A specific delivery instruction within a scheduling agreement — tells the supplier what to deliver and when.
Immediate
Deliveries due in the near term (today/this week) — highest urgency zone in the schedule.
GR via DCO
Some customers configure goods receipt to be triggered by the DCO delivery confirmation rather than a separate ASN.
Business Value

Pain Points DCO Solves

⚠ "Suppliers are surprised by delivery demands — only get last-minute call-offs"
JD transmits the full forecast horizon (8–26 weeks) giving suppliers visibility to plan capacity, order raw materials, and avoid panic deliveries.
⚠ "Buyers don't know if suppliers can meet the schedule until it's too late"
Suppliers confirm schedule lines in JD — exceptions and missed confirmations trigger automated alerts to buyers so they can react in time.
⚠ "Managing hundreds of scheduling agreements via spreadsheets is impossible"
JD aggregates all SA delivery schedules in one portal — suppliers see their full delivery plan across all agreements in a single view.
